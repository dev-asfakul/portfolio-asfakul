Siam Portfolio — Award-Grade Rebuild Prompt 


0. MISSION
You are a senior creative developer. Turn ASFAKUL SIAM — Digital Design House into a site worthy of Awwwards / FWA / CSSDA "Site of the Day".
Concept: One Identity. Multiple Moods. One CMS content model, three genuinely different designs (Quiet, Editorial, Play).
What is wrong today (the owner's words + audit):
1. Sections do not state their objective. A visitor cannot tell what this portfolio is, who it is for, or what to do next.
2. No connection between home sections and the pages /work, /about, /reviews, /contact. Header/footer are not a true shell.
3. Design work is not showcased clearly: no process, role, outcome or depth per project.
4. Animation feels like flat, old CSS transitions. GSAP is installed but barely used: no smooth scroll, no SplitText, no Flip, no batch, no 3D, no velocity effects, no shared-element transitions. Reverse/refresh is unreliable and scroll feels too fast.
5. Moods differ in colour and some layouts but share one font set, one nav, and inner pages ignore the mood.
6. Small screens are laggy and buggy.
Definition of done: a stranger understands who Siam is, what he makes, and sees proof in 5 seconds; every section answers why am I here / where next; every interaction has weight and feedback; it holds 60fps on mid-range phones; each mood is recognisable at a glance with the text blurred.

3.4 Motion & dimensionality language
The owner asked for "3D / 5D". There is no real 5D in a browser. Treat it as five axes of experience and design each deliberately:
1. X/Y — layout, masks, reveals.
2. Z (3D) — real depth: CSS 3D (perspective, rotateX/Y, translateZ) for cards/tilt, and one WebGL/Three.js moment per mood.
3. Time — scroll-scrubbed timelines, pinned sequences, scroll velocity.
4. Input — pointer position, magnetic hover, drag, keyboard, device tilt on mobile (opt-in).
5. State/Context — mood, route, reduced-motion, device capability.
Every animation must answer what does this communicate? No decoration for its own sake.
4. MOTION ENGINEERING SPEC
GSAP must be used as a system, not sprinkled. GSAP 3.13+ ships the former Club plugins free; confirm in node_modules/gsap and register in lib/motion/gsap.ts: ScrollTrigger, SplitText, Flip, Observer, CustomEase, DrawSVGPlugin, MorphSVGPlugin, MotionPathPlugin, ScrollToPlugin.
* Smooth scroll: Lenis, one instance, driven by gsap.ticker.add((t) => lenis.raf(t * 1000)), gsap.ticker.lagSmoothing(0), lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update). Feel: heavy and calm (lerp ≈ 0.08–0.1, wheel multiplier ≤ 1). Off for reduced motion; evaluate on touch devices and enable only if profiling proves smooth. Record the decision in an ADR.
* Tokens: lib/motion/tokens.ts — durations (0.18 / 0.35 / 0.7 / 1.1 s), staggers, and custom eases via CustomEase (siamOut, siamInOut, siamSpring). Moods override through MOODS[mood].motion.
* Media queries: FULL_MOTION = '(min-width: 768px) and (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)', HOVER = '(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)', REDUCED. Use gsap.matchMedia() everywhere; no ad-hoc innerWidth.
* Performance: animate only transform/opacity (and clip-path where profiled cheap); will-change only while animating; one ticker; kill/disable WebGL when off-screen or tab hidden; clamp WebGL DPR to 1.5; dynamic-import 3D code; cap active ScrollTriggers (target < 15 on mobile); use ScrollTrigger.batch for repeated items; quickTo for pointer follow.
* Reversibility: every from/fromTo gets immediateRender: false where needed; timelines tied to scroll use scrub (0.6–1.2) or toggleActions: 'play none none reverse'; invalidateOnRefresh: true; everything lives in useGSAP({ scope }) so unmount reverts. Test: scroll up, route back, resize, orientation change, hard refresh mid-page.
* Primitives to build in components/motion/ (reusable, mood-aware): SplitReveal (masked lines/words via SplitText), MaskImage (clip-path reveal + inner parallax), Magnetic, TiltCard (3D, quickTo), VelocitySkew (skew/stretch from scroll velocity), Marquee (velocity-reactive), PinnedSequence, HorizontalRail (containerAnimation), FlipGrid, DrawLine (DrawSVG), Counter, Cursor, Preloader, PageTransition, SectionProgress.
* 3D: three + @react-three/fiber (+ drei only if needed), lazy-loaded, desktop and capable-device only (navigator.hardwareConcurrency >= 6 and deviceMemory >= 4 or a quick fps probe), with a designed static/CSS-3D fallback. Pause on visibilitychange and when out of view. Budget: first-load JS unchanged for non-3D routes; 3D chunk < 180 kB gz.

7.3 Rules that make the story work
1. Continuity. One world, not stacked unrelated scenes. Shared elements (a line, a shape, a colour field, a type block) travel between acts via GSAP timelines and Flip.
2. Understandable without words. Mute test: a visitor reading no text should still say "this person designs and builds beautiful, high-end digital experiences."
3. Chapter navigation. A slim progress rail with act names (clickable, keyboard accessible, synced with Lenis/ScrollTrigger). A persistent, quiet "Start a project" appears after Act 3 and never blocks content.
4. Always an exit. Visitors can jump to Work or Contact at any time. Every act ends with a forward pointer and a link to its real page (/work, /about, /reviews, /contact). Inner pages continue the story: /work = evidence, /about = the person, /reviews = trust, /contact = invitation.
5. Pacing. Desktop: each act 1–1.5 viewports of pinned or scrubbed motion. Mobile: each act ≤ 1.5 screens, reduced to the strongest single idea per act. Total home scroll should feel like a short film, not a long page.
6. Mood changes the telling, not the plot. Same seven acts in every mood; each mood stages them differently (Quiet = slow and spacious, Editorial = precise and chaptered, Play = kinetic and surprising).
7. Reduced motion = a still storyboard of the same seven acts with the same order and links (designed, not degraded).
